# 12 COMMANDMENTS

Twelve clay tablets.
Each one the size and weight of a handheld device.
Each one fired around a stainless steel plate,
credit card sized,
engraved with a Shamir secret share,
completely enclosed.

The plate is only exposed if the tablet is broken.

---

## The Form

Each tablet is cast from a single mould —
a 10-inch form, palmable, substantial,
the proportions of the device
that has come to define contemporary life.

The front face is identical across all twelve.
Smooth fired clay. Unglazed. Raw.
No text. No image. No signature.
The surface of something very old
in the shape of something very new.

The reverse carries four raised dots —
two rows of two,
circular or rectangular,
pressed into the clay before firing.

They read as decoration.
They are binary.

```

1       0001    · · | · ●
```

The oldest writing surface humans ever used,
marked in the language of the machine.

---

## The Wallet

Inside each tablet, sealed in fired earth,
is a stainless steel plate engraved with twenty words.

The words are not the wallet.
They are one share of it.

The wallet is a single-signature Bitcoin address,
its seed split across all twelve tablets
using Shamir's Secret Sharing — SLIP39.

Each share reveals nothing alone.
Not a partial key. Not a clue.
Nothing.

The mathematics guarantees it.

The wallet becomes accessible only when
a sufficient number of tablets are broken
and their shares combined.
The threshold is known.
It is not published.

The public wallet address is public.
Anyone may watch the balance accumulate
via the mempool at any time.
The address is transparent.
The contents are sealed.

---

## The Installation

The twelve tablets are displayed together —
a complete set, a single work.

The tablets are silent.
No text. No label. No explanation on the objects themselves.

Alongside the installation, a single accompanying card:

```
12 COMMANDMENTS
Anonymous Art

[QR code — public wallet address]

IN CODE WE TRUST
```

Nothing more.

The QR code resolves to the public wallet address.
Anyone may look it up.
Anyone may watch the balance accumulate in real time.
Anyone may send sats to the wallet from their phone,
standing in front of the tablets,
contributing to something they cannot open.

The mempool is the living display.
The balance is part of the artwork's state.
It grows publicly, visibly, permanently.

For the Bitcoiner — the message is complete and precise.
The code is the protocol.
The trust is mathematical.
The commandments are not rules.
They are a secret distributed across twelve containers
that the institution will never break.

For everyone else — twelve ancient objects,
a familiar phrase slightly wrong,
a QR code that leads somewhere unexpected.

The artwork speaks differently to different audiences
without compromising itself for either.

## The Offer

12 COMMANDMENTS is not offered for sale.

It is offered to a museum —
as a complete set,
as a single work,
as a gift or a proposal.

The artist is anonymous.
There is no certificate signed by anyone.
There is no provenance beyond the objects themselves.
There is no one to contact for guidance or permission.

The institution receives twelve tablets,
the accompanying card,
and a document explaining what they contain.

---

## The Tension

The museum's purpose is preservation.
Every object in its collection is protected
from damage, alteration, and destruction.

12 COMMANDMENTS places an institution
dedicated to preserving objects
in permanent custody of a financial instrument
that can only be accessed
by destroying twelve irreplaceable things.

The Bitcoin inside accumulates.
The preservation instinct holds.
The tablets remain intact.

Each year that passes,
the value of breaking them grows.
Each year that passes,
the institution's mission holds firm
or it doesn't.

There is no third option.

---

## The Symbolism

Twelve tablets. Twelve tribes. Twelve seed words.
The number is not incidental.

The form is a handheld device —
the object that has come to mediate
every human transaction, communication, and distraction.
This one contains none of those things.
It contains only silence
and a secret that requires destruction to hear.

The binary on the reverse is the language
of every digital device ever made,
pressed into earth and fired at a thousand degrees.

A conservation team examining the surface markings
would find a writing system
that belongs to no known ancient civilisation.
It belongs to one invented in the 17th century
and ubiquitous from the 20th onwards.

The tablets are immediately and irreducibly contemporary
despite looking like they were excavated.

---

## The Question

Moses descended with two tablets
containing ten commandments.

These are twelve —
containing one instruction,
distributed,
accessible only through collective destruction,
held by an institution whose purpose
is to ensure that never happens.

The commandment is the wallet.
The wallet is the secret.
The secret is intact
for as long as the tablets are whole.

---

## The Making

The stainless steel plate is dipped four times in natural beeswax,
each coat allowed to set before the next.
The wax builds a protective skin around the engraved words —
a temporary guardian,
present only during creation.

The plate is embedded in earthenware clay,
cast in the mould,
completely enclosed.

The tablet dries for six days.

On the seventh day, it is kilned.

The firing runs for twelve hours —
one hour for each tablet,
one hour for each share,
one hour for each word on the plate inside.

During firing, the beeswax melts silently
and is absorbed into the surrounding clay.
By the time the kiln reaches temperature
the wax is gone entirely —
leaving a micro-thin void around the plate,
a breath of space between the metal and the earth.

The plate does not bond to the clay.
It floats inside it.
Sealed. Protected. Unreachable.

This is the lost wax technique —
cire perdue —
one of the oldest casting methods known to humans,
used for thousands of years to form bronze sculpture.
Here it is reversed.
The wax does not create the form.
It protects what is already formed
while the earth sets around it.

The making is the creation story.
It is known only to the artist.
The artist will not be identified.
The tablets carry no trace of the process
that brought them into being.

Only the result.
Twelve objects.
Six days. The seventh, fire.
Twelve hours.
The wax gone.
The words sealed inside.
The commandments intact.

*The artist is not present.*
*The tablets are the only authority.*
*In code we trust.*
