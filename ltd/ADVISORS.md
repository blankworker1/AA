# ADVISORS - AA Ltd

Advisors - known as The Witnesses

## Position within AA

The Witnesses are the advisory council of AA Ltd.
They do not manage the company. They witness, advise, and challenge.
Their authority is collective, not individual.

There are six, one per domain: legal/IP, compliance, tax, technical, curatorial, communications. Each Witness is invited for their domain knowledge, but operates within a shared conceptual frame.

---
## Advisory, not directors

The Witnesses are not directors of AA Ltd — executive or otherwise. UK company law draws no line between kinds of director: anyone appointed a director is filed at Companies House, named on the public record, identity-verified, and bound by the full duties of the office. The Witnesses are none of these things.

AA Ltd has one director: the [Director](DIRECTOR.md). The Witnesses sit beside the board, not on it.

- They are not appointed to the board and are not filed at Companies House.
- They have no vote, no authority to bind the company, and no power to instruct the Director.
- They advise; the Director decides and remains solely responsible for the decision.

The title is deliberate. A Witness is never called a director, and never acts as one. Anyone who in practice performs a director's functions, or whose instructions the Director habitually follows, may be treated as a director in law whatever they are called — so the advisory line is kept in conduct, not only in name.

## Core Responsibilities

Provide independent advice and challenge to the Director.

Advise on matters of risk, integrity, and long-term intent.

Represent a specific area of expertise (legal/IP, compliance, tax, technical, curatorial, communications).

Ensure that decisions remain aligned with the AA ethos, not short-term utility.

---
## Role within the Artwork

Witnesses are not employees; they are participants.

Their presence is part of the structure of the work itself.

Each Witness acts as a living constraint, preventing collapse into commerce, spectacle, or excess.


They do not produce artworks.
They protect the conditions under which artworks exist.


---
## Governance & Power

Witnesses hold no executive power.

They cannot issue shares, call capital, or direct or override the Director.

As a group, they may:

Request explanations

Record dissent

Ask the Director for a formal review

Their influence is procedural, not performative. Whether to act on it is the Director's decision, answerable to the shareholders.

## Treasury signatories

Two of the six Witnesses also serve as **Treasury signatories**, holding two of the three keys to the company's [Treasury](TREASURY.md) multisig. Which two is settled among the Witnesses themselves; the [Director](DIRECTOR.md) holds the third.

Signing is intended to be ministerial, not a decision. A signatory signs only to give effect to a movement already authorised, having verified it against its mandate, and withholds a signature only when a movement is unauthorised or the keys or process look compromised. The verification is a procedural check; it is not a veto on the merits, and no one — the Director included — can move the Treasury alone.

Holding a key does carry duties the advisory role alone does not: secure the key, verify before signing, stay reachable, and hand the key over in a controlled way before stepping down. In this one respect a signatory is not free to walk away without notice.

> **Open question for the legal Witness — unresolved.**
> A Witness who holds a Treasury key can, with one other signatory, block or enable a movement of company funds. Does that co-signing function risk the key-holding Witnesses being treated as de facto or shadow directors of AA Ltd — and so needing to be appointed, named and filed — despite the advisory framing above? If so, what keeps the role advisory: a written signing mandate, key-holders who are not Witnesses, or a different custody design? Until this is answered, the Treasury signatory arrangement is a proposal, not a settled structure.

## Visibility & Anonymity

Witnesses may remain anonymous or pseudonymous to the public, by choice. Because they are not directors, no public filing names them.

They are known to the Director. A formal written opinion from a regulated professional will carry that professional's name; it is a private document of the company.

Public recognition is not required; contribution outweighs attribution.

Their role is to stabilize the system, not to promote themselves.

---
## Term & Commitment

Invitation is non-fixed, aligned with the 4-year AA cycle.

Service is voluntary, symbolic, and intellectual rather than operational.

A Witness may step down at any time without explanation — except a Treasury signatory, who must first hand over their key in a controlled way (see Treasury signatories).

## Ideal Witness Profile

Trusted, independent thinker.

Comfortable operating in ambiguity and constraint.

Understands that AA is not a company pretending to be art, but art using the company form.

the Witnesses are the Council.

If the shareholders are the Chosen,

Together, they ensure AA remains coherent, rare, and resistant to decay.
